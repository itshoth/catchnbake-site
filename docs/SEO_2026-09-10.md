# Search clicks and version 1.1.0

September 10, 2026

## Evidence

The owner's September 9 Search Console export covers August 10 through September 6. It reports 6 clicks from 2,135 page impressions. The snook guide accounts for 2,045 impressions, 6 clicks, 0.29% CTR, and average position 9.25. Mobile accounts for all six clicks. The query “can you eat snook” has 323 impressions, no clicks, and average position 9.45. “Can you eat snook in florida” has 116 impressions, one click, and average position 7.25.

This is the baseline before the September 9 title, introduction, article, and photograph changes. It cannot measure those changes. Low CTR here may reflect position, competing answers, or the search listing. The export alone does not establish a single cause.

## Changes

Added eight visible homepage FAQs, with links to relevant fish guides and support. The homepage description now states the free monthly identification allowance. The opening states photo identification, catch logging, and species recipes in plain language. Native HTML disclosures work without JavaScript. The new updates page and FAQ have footer links across the site.

Kept the September 9 snook title and introduction stable so the next measurement has a useful comparison. No keyword volume, ranking improvement, or competitor superiority is claimed.

The 1.1.0 page is a preview. Evidence comes from the task “Review Catch 'N Bake project” and the app repository's `docs/app-store/1.1.0/STORE_COPY.md`, `en-US/whats-new.txt`, and `docs/REVAMP_REVIEW.md`. Those records support the feed, follows, profiles, filters, shared catch rankings, cookbook, shopping lists, cooking mode, and repaired forms. They do not establish public availability. Canadian app regulations were not found in the reviewed app files. The homepage FAQ describes the existing Pacific halibut website guide and its DFO links, without claiming Canadian coverage in the app.

## FAQ and structured data

Google discontinued FAQ rich results on May 7, 2026 and removed the documentation in June. Visible answers can still help readers; FAQ markup is not a route to extra search listings. The updates page uses WebPage structured data. Existing article and breadcrumb markup remains intact. No review scores or Recipe markup were invented.

Sources: [Google documentation updates](https://developers.google.com/search/updates), [title guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/title-link), and [snippet guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/snippet).
